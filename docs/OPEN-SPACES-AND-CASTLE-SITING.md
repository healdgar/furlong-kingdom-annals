# Open spaces and castle siting

Recorded 2026-10-10 before implementation, following the user's review of the round, evergreen village greens and repeated motte-and-bailey layouts.

## Historical basis

A village green was shared open ground among the settlement's roads and frontages. Grazing, gathering and common facilities such as a well or pond could coexist there. It is distinct from the wider commons, whose importance lies in rights to pasture, wood and other resources. Greens were not uniformly round: Historic England records an irregular green with houses along its edge at [Manfield](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1017802), and a triangular green/market at a track junction at [Much Cowarne](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1021001). Regional planned villages could have rectangular greens. Markets could use a widened street, a junction or a formal planned square; frontage and access mattered more than a standard outline.

A motte was the defended mound; the bailey was a working enclosed yard, normally beside it. Terrain, existing banks and ditches, access and the household's needs varied the arrangement. [Pulborough](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1017547) has a west bailey, a north-east approach and a steep slope without a ditch on one side. A curved terraced approach is plausible ([Dorstone](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1014545)); a full spiral road is not a universal plan. Halls, domestic quarters, stores, workshops and stables belonged to the working castle; chapels and further yards varied with development. The existing keep, hall and longhouse remain actual institutions, rather than adding decorative buildings without actors or costs.

## Dependency order and implementation boundary

1. Read buildable terrain, water, reserved road widths, standing footprints and churchyards. Fit the initial open-space reservation before building frontages.
2. Preserve the existing canonical radial boundary format. A pure `placeFitRadii` scans the whole space on fixed rays, stops at the first obstruction and constrains the interpolated boundary too. The fitted space reserves land for the usual well or market furniture.
3. After initial town fabric and access lanes exist, fit a modest expansion against those actual boundaries. Update its hidden boundary street from the same radii. Never expand over a road, standing footprint, churchyard, another place or fort reservation. Saved layouts keep their recorded shape; there is no daily resurvey or draw-dependent layout.
4. Survey a bailey's usable courtyard against terrain, roads and protected buildings. Road proximity uses the existing settlement segment index with the same centre-line clearance test; no new maintained index or full street scan per ray sample. Use the surveyed courtyard plus mound to construct the existing convex enclosure representation; reject an enclosure that encloses unusable land. Preserve convexity because parcel and curtain consumers require it.
5. Test graded mound approaches with zero through one turn in either direction, and select the shortest feasible route. The canonical street follows that surveyed path; wall, movement and render consumers read the same geometry. Derive the gate from the actual enclosure radius. Survey and retain internal/external access detours around the future hall, longhouse and existing standing footprints before accepting the site.
6. Project these canonical boundaries through the existing worker geometry packet. Render green vertices with an explicit grass tag using the existing grass, dormancy and snow uniforms. Roads and markets have a zero grass tag. Season changes neither simulation geometry nor random streams.

These are intentional changes to generated layouts and subsequent history. They must remain deterministic across speeds and save continuation. The seasonal shader is presentation only. The retained twenty-ray open-space format cannot represent holes or disconnected land; an obstructed centre rejects a site instead of painting through it. Court fitting is bounded at founding, never part of daily simulation. More bailey types, stairs/bridges and paid service buildings remain further institution/development work.

## First-castle development gap

The user's follow-up on 2026-10-10 exposed a separate missing operation (#60). Fresh generated `seat`/`fort` layouts can place an initial keep. Later `tickCastles` returns when both `casKeep(s)` and `casRing(s)` are absent; `casNext` develops existing keeps/curtains and `rebuildKeep` only replaces an existing keep. Moving a lord's seat or promoting a settlement to royal capital therefore cannot commission its first keep. Town walls can arise independently through `tickHistory` and its actual murage funds.

First-castle construction must assess the current seat, capital or defensive role and its protection value against cost, then select and retain a viable terrain/fabric/access site. Resolve the ruler and funds before committing land; buy real materials and pay real labour through saved construction progress. Recheck changed ownership, siege and site obstruction before starting or completing work. This is a further simulation operation, not part of the current fresh-layout/seasonal fix, and must not be implemented by rerunning settlement generation or creating an unfunded decorative castle.

Read-only dependency audit:

- `casLvl` already distinguishes the royal capital, a non-exiled house's actual seat, explicit seat/fort roles and lesser places. Every settlement has an owner; that alone is not a foundation decision. Use current `s.owner`, `purse`, `purseAcct` and `houseAcct`, rather than a new treasury.
- The deterministic first-site survey is currently local to `layoutSettlement`. Expose a dry survey/quote through the live layout API; retain its selected terrain, court, footprint and access geometry only with a complete input certificate. Do not rerun random hill-castle generation or the whole settlement layout daily.
- Existing `tickCastles` mutators apply structural upgrades immediately, then fund labour/materials and possibly start `wallWorks`. Wall progress alone does not provide first-keep construction progress. Add a native persisted foundation project to `W.projects`, progressed by the daily project phase. Endogenous AI proposals need no synthetic player command; save/replay must derive the same proposal from the same day and inputs.
- Pay actual labour through `buildWorks`, consume purchased timber/stone through `buyBuildingMaterial`, and stall for funds, stock, siege, exile or an obstructed site. Specify rates and completion before replacing the current instantaneous operations. Foundation and later upgrades are distinct proposals, with one pending project suppressing duplicate proposals.
- Completion creates canonical buildings and motte/bailey or keep/ward data, updates `fortPrepare`, layout/parcel/route state and the existing worker geometry projection. `casKeep`, `casRing`, fort circuits and garrison need must see the same objects that the renderer draws.
- Gate eligibility and duplicate suppression, changed owners/sites, paid-day stalls, money/inventory conservation, native save continuation and speed identity before release. Later existing castle upgrades remain a separate annual decision until their own daily rate conversion.

## Validation

Focused obstruction/interpolation/access/season tests pass. Native castle access streets are checked against every standing building footprint at boot. Two 150-day comparisons pass: indexed versus full-scan road clearance, then operation-local valuation versus the unbatched candidate. Each also compares steady speed with all seven speed settings, complete logical world/global/layout/RNG state at days 0/37/90/150, daily RNG/commands/annals/sequence and all 1,812 accepted raw storage events. No authoritative value is normalized; only playback preferences and asynchronous chunk packing are excluded.

The initial drawn summer/winter review loads the atlas and has no shader/runtime errors. The final suite verifies 1,517/1,518 checks; its one unchanged 30-second VM full-world graph capture timeout under parallel load passes in isolation. Both one-year native browser worlds pass all money/inventory/sanity checks with no simulation faults. Final worker/save, stamped drawn review and release evidence with source hashes are recorded in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md). New layout shapes apply to fresh layouts; saved boundaries remain intact.
