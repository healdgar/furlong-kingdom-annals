# Castle foundation survey: inputs and reuse limits

This note records the dependency audit for the first-foundation site survey in `index.html`. It documents the implemented conservative AABB broad phase and bounded optimization ideas; the frontage precheck and scalar broad-phase refinements proposed below are not implemented.

## Current call and cache boundary

`castleFoundationProposal` (`index.html:7080`) applies current authority, siege, patron, keep/ring, and pending-project checks; selects the current-era keep specification; then checks the current purse, town value, and threat before calling `castleFoundationSite`. It calculates land opportunity cost and final cost/net only after the site has been surveyed. `castleFoundationCommission` (`:7091`) stores the resulting fixed site and quote in a project. `tickCastles` (`:10846`) attempts a first foundation for an eligible seat on each daily tick; upgrades remain annual. A retained project is passed to `castleFoundationTick` (`:7095`), which calls `castleFoundationSite(s, q.spec, q.site)` again before work, materials, or payment each paid day.

`castleFoundationSite` (`:7011`) intentionally makes fresh operation-local building and segment hashes (`B`/`H`), a per-call `WeakMap` for building bounds, and current arrays of anchors, protected ground, nearby fort circuits, and projects. Its broad phases are followed by the existing exact geometry predicates. Nothing in this helper memoizes a site result across calls or days. A proposed foundation keeps its location fixed; a later obstruction stalls the project rather than relocating it.

## Inputs that make cross-day reuse unsafe today

The survey's result depends on all of the following live inputs:

- **Frontage and urban geometry:** every settlement's live buildings and graveyard adjuncts (coordinates, dimensions, rotation, polygons/lots, removal/state); streets and their points/widths/kinds plus `gone`, `hidden`, `planQ`, `castlePath`, and `serviceLinked` flags; current open-road paths; and `trackSet` edges resolved through `W.tg.E` and `W.tg.pts`. Candidate anchors also depend on the subject's current street list and the public-frontage eligibility rules in `castleFoundationSite`.
- **Protected and reserved geometry:** places, citadels, remnants, burg rings, relic-wall segments, and fort circuits including each current wall-radius array; plus non-dead projects and their mutable road paths, plan streets/squares/walls, castle sites, storage sites, and clear areas. The retained project is excluded from the project-obstacle list by its current site identity.
- **Ground and title:** `planCellOK`, `riverAt`, `lakeAt`, `hAt`, `furlongAt`, `castleFoundationLandOK`, current field/domain/lord/title/owner/tenure/croft values, and the occupied-ground mask. `castleFoundationGround` and `occupiedGroundCells` apply whole-cell land checks for the retained footprint/ward. The site's score and approach grade also depend on terrain height. `spec` changes with the era.

Relevant writers are distributed rather than routed through one layout revision. `layoutSettlement` (`:2326`) reconstructs settlement geometry and creates `addStreet`/placement closures. Its live `stopUp` and `planOpen` operations change street status (`:3265-3267`); later growth can expose hidden streets (`:11650-11659`). Road paths are replaced by `stitchRoads` (`:3412`); track membership is consumed by the live `fromTracks` path (`:3131-3138`) and rebuilt by `settleTracks` (`:11213`, assignment at `:11257`). Churchyard geometry is changed in `graveyardSetShape` (`:8761`), and completed churchyards update the occupied mask through `markOccupiedGround` (`:8812`). Field state changes go through `setFurlong` (`:8269`), land rights through `setLord` (`:11270`) and `syncOwner`, and domain assignments through `computeDomains` (`:11023`). Walls/circuits are changed by `setRectWall` (`:2204`), `fortPrepare` (`:2242`), live wall planning (`:3269-3275`), and plan execution (`:10904`). Projects are appended by their various commission paths, including castle commissioning at `:7091`; project completion, cancellation, and plan execution mutate project and site state elsewhere.

These writers include in-place edits, same-count moves, and derived state. There is no common revision counter covering the complete dependency set. Array/object identity alone therefore misses in-place mutations; a hash alone is not a proof of equality. Exact cross-day reuse would require a collision-free comparison of a complete, current dependency snapshot, including the sampled environmental and land predicates, and proof that every relevant writer and load/worker reconstruction updates or invalidates it. Rechecking only the retained site's coordinates, town street count, or project count is insufficient. Daily financial eligibility, land opportunity, materials, and work conditions must remain live even if a future complete geometry certificate were established.

## Bounded proposals (not implemented)

1. **Necessary frontage guard before index construction.** No candidate can be formed without a current eligible public segment from the subject's own streets. A bounded first pass can collect those segments using the existing rules (street is not gone or edge; it is not hidden, planned, or castle-path; a quay must be service-linked; segment length exceeds one metre). If there are none, return `null` before building `B`, `H`, or collecting unrelated geometry; otherwise reuse the collected anchors in the normal survey. This is an exact current-state rejection, with no cache or retry policy. Its benefit is limited to settlements without eligible frontage; it does not address failures where frontage exists but all candidate ground is blocked.

2. **Conservative scalar point-to-box broad phase.** `castleFoundationBoxMayHit` (`:7005`) currently allocates an array for `.every(Number.isFinite)` and calls `Math.hypot(dx,dz)` for each AABB test. A candidate kernel can check the seven scalar inputs directly, keep the current fail-open behavior for invalid values, and first reject when either axis separation exceeds `pad + 1e-6` (because Euclidean distance cannot then be within the pad). A squared-distance comparison may avoid `Math.hypot` for clearly separated points, but it must be conservative: float rounding must not skip a point that the current `Math.hypot(dx,dz) <= pad + 1e-6` accepts. Use an outward error allowance and call the original `Math.hypot` predicate for near-boundary/overflow cases; extra broad-phase hits are safe because exact geometry remains authoritative. Validate the kernel against the original predicate around boundaries, zero padding, invalid/non-finite inputs, and very large finite coordinates before adopting it.

The profile motivating the review was an instrumented AD1066 sample reporting `tickCastles` at about **200.685 sampled CPU ms/day** and `castleFoundationSite` at about **202.126 sampled CPU ms/day**. Those are profiler sample costs, not measured throughput or wall-clock time per simulation day. The profiled `castleFoundationBoxMayHit` self/total and geometry-predicate costs make the scalar broad-phase idea a bounded target, not a claim that it resolves the whole site-survey cost.
